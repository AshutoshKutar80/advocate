Legal Guide

Know your rights before you need them.

Short, practical primers on situations people commonly face.

If you are arrested

Stay calm, cooperate physically, and exercise your constitutional safeguards.

  1. You must be told the grounds of your arrest.
  2. You may inform a relative or friend and consult a lawyer of your choice.
  3. You must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours, excluding travel time.
  4. Do not sign blank or unread papers, and do not give a statement without legal advice.
  5. You are entitled to a medical examination, and to be treated without violence or humiliation.

In an emergency call our urgent line at+91 98765 43210.

Cheque bounced

Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act works to strict timelines.

  1. A cheque is valid for three months from its date; present it within that period.
  2. After the bank's return memo, send a written demand notice within 30 days.
  3. The drawer then has 15 days from receiving the notice to pay.
  4. If unpaid, the complaint must be filed within one month after those 15 days expire.
  5. Keep the cheque, return memo, notice and postal proof safely; missing a step can end the case.

Accused persons also have defences. Get advice before replying to any notice.

Tenant & landlord

A clear written agreement prevents most rental disputes.

  1. Always have a written rent agreement; tenancies over 11 months generally require registration.
  2. Record the security deposit, rent increase, notice period and lock-in terms precisely.
  3. A landlord cannot lawfully evict by force; eviction needs due process.
  4. Keep rent receipts or bank transfer proof for every payment.
  5. Photograph the property at move-in and move-out to avoid deduction disputes.

Rules differ by state and by whether a rent control law applies.

Divorce basics

Understand the two main routes before taking a decision.

  1. Mutual consent divorce suits couples who agree on separation, maintenance and custody.
  2. It generally needs a period of separation before filing, and a cooling-off period that courts can sometimes waive.
  3. Contested divorce requires a legal ground such as cruelty or desertion, proved with evidence.
  4. Maintenance and child custody are decided on welfare and financial need, not as punishment.
  5. Settlement through mediation is often faster, cheaper and less painful than prolonged litigation.

Personal laws vary by religion and the Special Marriage Act may apply.
